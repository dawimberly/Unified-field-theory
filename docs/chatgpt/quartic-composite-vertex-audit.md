## Decision

**DERIVED** Investigate a narrow extension of the scalar composite-gravity model: an independent O(N)-invariant quartic term in the constituent potential, distinct from the derivative interactions already generated by the composite action. Begin with the symmetric vacuum and weak quartic coupling, leading large N, and a specified covariant regulator. Determine the spin-2 two-point kernel with the required tadpole, contact and vertex terms included.

**DERIVED** The central question is whether a healthy massless spin-2 pole survives this extension and how its residue changes after the background condition is imposed. Merely obtaining the familiar answer from a supplied Einstein operator would not count. Neither would inserting a shifted mass into the old free-constituent kernel without establishing that omitted diagrams vanish.

## Concrete groundwork completed

**DERIVED** We derived the additional tree-level six-field terms implied by the exact $D=4$ determinant action after introducing a quartic potential. The coefficients were checked against the exact algebraic expression on twelve generic configurations, using 60-digit decimal arithmetic to avoid subtraction error. Correct truncation errors scale approximately as amplitude$^{8}$; omitting the new terms gives amplitude$^{6}$ errors. Ninety-six implementation checks pass. This is interaction bookkeeping, not a quantum pole calculation or evidence that the model describes nature.

**DERIVED** The simplified tree-background coefficient is V0 and the normal-ordering/counterterm structure is deliberately not implemented in this algebra check. Consequently its vertices are a preliminary ingredient to be embedded in a fully specified renormalized calculation, not ready-to-use complete quantum Feynman rules.

## Next calculation, stated precisely

**DERIVED** Take $V(\phi )=V0+(m0^{2}/2)\phi ^{2}+\lambda (\phi ^{2})^{2}/(4N)$. Work to first order in the independent lambda and leading order in $1/N$, with a regulator and counterterm prescription that preserve coordinate covariance. This changes a theory parameter; it does NOT yet describe a binding reaction or a state transition at fixed Hamiltonian.

1. **DERIVED** Specify the complete action and the regulator's extension to the interacting theory, including which fields couple to which interactions. Choose a consistent counterterm basis and background condition. A covariant proper-time regulator used for an unrelated induced-gravity control is not automatically an implementation of the composite theory's measure or constraint.
2. **DERIVED** List all diagrams at the chosen orders: constituent self-energy, stress-vertex corrections, local second metric variations/contact terms, and the extra vertices identified in the expansion. Account for large-N factors. Establish rather than assume which terms vanish after the spin-2 projection.
3. **DERIVED** Verify the interacting conservation/Ward identities with the background/tadpole condition included. Separate a flat-background expansion failure from a genuine physical graviton mass or a ghost pole.
4. **DERIVED** Compute the spin-2 kernel and the scalar sector. Test pole position, residue sign, unwanted poles and universal matter coupling in that specified approximation.
5. **DERIVED** Only then compare with prior work and ask whether the result is new. An additional interaction may merely renormalize an existing coefficient, or may render the approximation inconsistent. Either outcome should be reported plainly.

## Criteria for a useful result

**DERIVED** Success at this stage is a reproducible analytic calculation that resolves the stated kernel question under explicit assumptions. An exact derivation showing that leading corrections reduce to a known mass renormalization could be useful, but is not automatically publishable. A failure must identify the specific identity, mode or approximation that fails. Fitting a chosen G, counting code checks, or reproducing an established curved-vacuum result is insufficient.

**DERIVED** The larger objective remains explaining quantum-to-Einstein behavior. The candidate model assumes substantial coordinate and geometric structure; even a successful extension would not explain that structure from a probability substrate, reproduce the Standard Model, fix G, solve the cosmological constant problem, or establish a Nobel-level result.
