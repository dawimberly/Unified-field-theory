## A: volume responds to ordering, with both signs tested
**DERIVED** Use a canonical Einstein-frame volume modulus sigma and canonical order field q, reduced reference Planck mass 1:

**DERIVED** $S = integral sqrt(-g) [\frac{R}{2} - (partial sigma)^\frac{2}{2} - (partial q)^\frac{2}{2} - U] + S_m[A(sigma)^2 g, matter].$

**DERIVED** c=sqrt[n(n+2)/2], radius/radius0=exp(sigma/c), A=exp[-n sigma/(2c)]. This is the usual isotropic toroidal volume mode with matter masses fixed in the Jordan metric. The stabilization potential is a chosen phenomenological addition, not derived from higher-dimensional fluxes or a string compactification:

**DERIVED** $U=\frac{k}{2} (sigma-a q^2)^2 + \frac{lambda}{4} (q^2-v^2)^2.$

**DERIVED** At q=+/-v, sigma=a v^2. All scanned k>0 and lambda=1 give a positive Hessian, determinant 2 k lambda v^2. The disordered q=sigma=0 point is unstable; ratios use its coupling as a reference, not a second stable phase. Both stable ordered signs have the same volume. Vacuum energy is set to zero by construction; radiative stability is untested.

**DERIVED** Tensor coupling in physical matter units is proportional to A^2=exp(-n sigma/c). Positive a expands internal dimensions and weakens tensor gravity. Negative a shrinks them and strengthens it. Either sign is locally stable: stability does not choose the sign.

**DERIVED** The radion mixes with q. Diagonalizing the complete 2x2 Hessian gives canonical masses m_i and couplings beta_i=-n/(2c) times the sigma component of eigenvector i. For weak, unscreened nonrelativistic test bodies, the extra force divided by tensor force is sum_i 2 beta_i^2 (1+m_i r) exp(-m_i r). Here r is Einstein-frame distance and masses are Einstein-frame masses. Comparison across vacua at fixed physical distance requires rescaling r by A. Short-distance unsuppressed sum is n/(n+2), between 2/3 and 4/5 over the dimensions scanned. These are substantial extra attractions, not automatically viable. Stabilizing the volume can shorten their range; mixing can leave a lighter coupled mode. No laboratory units, screening solution, or observational likelihood is supplied.

**DERIVED** For a=+0.5, the tensor ratio is 0.5614 at D=8 and 0.5313 at D=12. For a=-0.5, it is 1.7813 and 1.8822. These are chosen-parameter responses, not predictions. The dimension count alone barely distinguishes these examples. For the smaller positive shift a=0.1 the extra scalar force reverses the sign of the TOTAL sufficiently-short-distance change despite the weaker tensor coupling; results.json records all distances.

## B: ordering changes the curvature coefficient directly
**DERIVED** An independent Jordan action uses F(q)R/2, F=1+xi q^2, a positive canonical Jordan kinetic term, and Jordan potential F^2(q)(q^2-1)^2/4. The F^2 factor deliberately makes the Einstein potential a double well. This is an assumed action, not a derived ordering law.

**DERIVED** At q=1, positive xi weakens the tensor coupling 1/F; negative xi strengthens it while F remains positive. F<=0 is rejected: the tensor kinetic term is singular or has the wrong sign. After conformal transformation the scalar kinetic coefficient is K=1/F+3(F'/F)^2/2, and beta=-F'/(2 F sqrt(K)). The scalar adds attractive exchange proportional to beta squared, irrespective of the sign of xi. Thus flipping a coupling sign does not make healthy scalar exchange repulsive. The dynamical field equations, including derivatives of F, are required; merely inserting G(q) into Einstein's equation is not equivalent to this action.

## C: density changes the extra force rather than fundamental tensor gravity
**DERIVED** The known symmetron mechanism has V_eff=(rho/M^2-mu^2)phi^2/2+lambda phi^4/4 with A approximately 1+phi^2/(2M^2). High density restores phi=0 and suppresses the leading scalar coupling. Low density allows nonzero phi and an additional attractive force. The scan tests homogeneous minima in units mu=M=lambda=1. The force estimate is a leading small-field formula, qualitative for phi/M near one. At the critical density the quadratic mass vanishes; this is marginal, not strictly positive stability. Real extended bodies require nonlinear boundary-value solutions and screening factors; density alone is not a complete experimental prediction.

**DERIVED** This mechanism can make attraction decrease as density rises without changing the Einstein tensor coefficient. Density and order are distinct variables; here higher density destroys this particular symmetry-breaking order.

## D: a new repulsive force after ordering
**DERIVED** A healthy massive vector can produce repulsive Yukawa exchange between like charges. Take V(r)=-G m1 m2/r + g^2 Q1 Q2 exp(-r/L)/(4 pi r). The inward acceleration relative to ordinary gravity is 1-alpha z_source z_test (1+r/L)exp(-r/L), with normalized charge-to-mass ratios z. We scan alpha=0.01..2, order=0,0.5,1, and distances r/L=0.01,1,10, setting z_source=z_test=order for the reference body. This is an imposed effective charge law, not a derivation of vector charge creation. At alpha=0.5 and full order, the long-range approximation halves attraction. At alpha>1 it can reverse it. A 0.1 percent test-body charge-to-mass mismatch produces a nonzero differential acceleration; near cancellation the fractional mismatch is amplified. This would be a constraint only if the field reaches the experiment, the source carries charge and screening is absent.

**DERIVED** The most important consistency problem is not force cancellation: it is obtaining the assumed charge law. In a conserved U(1) theory, binding cannot create net vector charge; charges add. Binding can change mass, charge distribution, effective screening or mediator mass. Turning order-dependent charge on by hand therefore does not supply a complete gauge-consistent model. A neutral body remains neutral after binding unless compensating charge goes elsewhere. Photons neutral under this new vector would not receive the same direct force as massive charged bodies; lensing and motion would then differ. The vector field's own stress-energy also gravitates; this leading weak-field scan neglects that backreaction. No claim of gravitational shielding is made.

## Other forces and radiation
**DERIVED** For universally conformally coupled scalars the leading source is the stress-energy trace. Nonrelativistic matter sources the scalar; classical electromagnetic radiation in four dimensions has zero trace and does not source it in the same way, even though its stress-energy still gravitates through Einstein's equation. Trace anomalies, binding-energy contributions and nonconformal direct couplings require additional calculations. This suite does not simulate electromagnetic or nuclear fields and does not claim their effects are absent.

## What the comparison decides
**ASSUMED** Both signs of ordering-induced tensor changes can be embedded in locally stable, covariant toy actions. None is selected by ordering or dimensionality alone. Healthy scalar exchange adds attraction; reduced observed attraction can instead arise by suppressing an existing scalar force. A change in tensor coupling, a change in force range, and screening are experimentally different hypotheses. Ordinary binding-energy mass loss remains a separate baseline.
