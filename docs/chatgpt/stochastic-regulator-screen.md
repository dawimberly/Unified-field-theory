## Three discriminating checks from existing results

### 1. Lorentz-invariant discreteness: retain, with qualifications

**CITED** Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem, Modern Physics Letters A 24 (2009), 2579-2587, DOI 10.1142/S0217732309031958, supplies an established result about Poisson sprinklings in Minkowski space. Under the theorem's measurable/equivariant construction assumptions, a sprinkling cannot intrinsically select a spacetime direction. The paper also excludes an associated Lorentz-invariant finite-valency graph. Thus a fixed nearest-neighbor lattice is not an interchangeable implementation of the proposed stochastic events. The theorem addresses kinematics, not gravitational dynamics or unitarity. — eq./page UNVERIFIED.

### 2. Deriving quantum mechanics: do not assume local stochastic equations suffice

**CITED** Wallstrom, Inequivalence between the Schrodinger equation and the Madelung hydrodynamic equations, Physical Review A 49 (1994), 1613, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.49.1613, identifies a missing quantization condition in reconstructing quantum mechanics from the hydrodynamic equations. The publisher abstract was inspected; its full proof was not retrieved. This is a required test for a claimed stochastic derivation, not a demonstrated refutation of Erlich's specific model. — eq./page UNVERIFIED.

**CITED** Our applicability inference: constructing a stochastic drift from an already admissible quantum wavefunctional is different from deriving the global quantum state space. The 2025 essay explicitly writes its drift in terms of a wavefunctional's phase and amplitude; that reverse construction does not by itself settle the forward derivation. ### 3. Flat equilibrium: do not promote it into a full spectrum result

**DERIVED** The displayed equilibrium evidence in the early stochastic work is not a calculation of the complete gauge-invariant spin-0 and spin-2 spectrum of the regulated model. To connect to our previous scalar-pole concern, the actual regulated, source-matched response must be derived. Substituting a cutoff scale into the old dimensionally continued kernel is not such a derivation. Nor does an equilibrium metric expectation prove that all perturbations have positive norm and stable time evolution.
