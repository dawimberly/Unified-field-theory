# Field-transition collapse: cross-reference assessment
1 November 2026. Working hypothesis assessment for Dan Wimberly.

## Result
The pond-crest analogy gives the original resonant-lock proposal a sharper candidate mechanism: a physical field instability activated by local concentration or gradients. Existing project results do not demonstrate objective collapse, and several directly prevent identifying ordinary field reorganization with collapse. The most useful next work is a single-particle, two-location test, rather than another composite-gravity pole scan.

This is a targeted cross-reference of accessible research, not a rereading of every historical chat or a new simulation of the full theory. Reviewed sources include the current October 30 synthesis; the original Possibility Cloud notes; Candidate 0; amplitude dynamics and its static audit; knotted-field dynamics; the binding-mode report; scalar threshold continuation; ordering/gravity comparison; the quantum-to-Einstein audit; and the Penrose transcript read in this conversation. Some combined tool outputs were truncated; conclusions below rely on visible passages and the full current synthesis. The morphic-resonance discussion is available as conversation context, not a completed physical model.

## The proposed idea, preserved accurately
A single particle has a physically extended underlying field. Smooth evolution can cross a local instability threshold, analogous to a wave crest breaking. Candidate triggers include field magnitude, gradients, energy concentration and persistence. Gravity might determine or influence the threshold. Neither a probability interpretation nor gravitational control is assumed proven. A radius from a chosen center is a diagnostic option, not yet a universal law.

## A concrete calculation already available in Candidate 0
In the amplitude-only sector, U(rho)=lambda(rho^2-v^2)^2/4. Therefore

    U''(rho)=lambda(3 rho^2-v^2).

For lambda>0 the potential has negative local curvature when rho<v/sqrt(3). In a homogeneous, momentarily frozen amplitude background, a linear Fourier perturbation has

    omega_k^2=k^2+lambda(3 rho^2-v^2).

Only modes with omega_k^2<0 have instantaneous exponential growth in this diagnostic. At rho=v, U''=2 lambda v^2>0. At rho=0, U''=-lambda v^2. These follow directly by differentiating the existing action; no numerical simulation is claimed.

This is an important countercheck: the existing simple potential has its negative-curvature region at LOW amplitude, not an arbitrarily large crest. Large amplitude alone increases its local restoring curvature. This does not rule out gradient-driven or coupled-field instabilities, nor prove dynamical instability for an evolving inhomogeneous background. At the polar boundary rho=0, a regular field parametrization is needed. It does show that the proposed high-crest effect is not already established by this potential.

## The two-location obstruction
Suppose one particle has two identical, separated field lobes. A deterministic local threshold based only on identical peak properties treats both lobes identically. With reflection-symmetric initial data and unique symmetric equations, reflection symmetry persists. Both peaks can change, or neither can; the rule alone does not choose one.

An instability can amplify tiny asymmetry, but then the source, statistics and physical status of that asymmetry matter. Numerical roundoff choosing a lobe is not an explanation of measurement probabilities. A completed model must yield one full detection and the observed distribution over repeated preparations, including unequal lobe weights. State-dependent nonlinear quantum dynamics also requires an explicit no-signalling check; nonlinearity alone is not a safe solution.

## How to formulate the next test
1. Retain a broad isolated single-particle state and two separated alternatives as distinct benchmarks. Also retain a stationary bound state: localization should not automatically count as a detection event.
2. Specify the underlying field and its energy/current. Do not equate rho, |psi|^2, mass density and coherence by notation.
3. Compare amplitude-only, gradient-sensitive and gravitationally controlled triggers as separate hypotheses. Define spatial resolution and duration; a threshold at a single unresolved lattice point is unreliable.
4. If using u=T_ab v^a v^b, define the physical timelike velocity v^a. Energy density is frame dependent; an observer-dependent number cannot silently become an observer-independent collapse law. For charged fields, use gauge-invariant quantities and covariant derivatives as appropriate.
5. Locate a genuine stability boundary using the fluctuation equations. Growing perturbations are evidence of instability, not yet evidence of collapse.
6. Track all outgoing energy, momentum and conserved charge. If gravity remains near Einstein dynamics, total stress-energy must satisfy the required conservation equation, including any collapse-related sector.
7. Run symmetric and slightly asymmetric two-lobe controls, vary separation, and test unequal weights. Measure one-versus-two-event statistics and recovery of interference below threshold.
8. Only after those gates, test entangled preparations for no-signalling and compare collapse rates with gravitational self-energy scaling and experiment.

A threshold inserted by hand and programmed to select outcomes would illustrate the assumption; it would not discover its mechanism. No new full solver or physical threshold value is justified by the present evidence.

## Changed research priority
The earlier synthesis prioritized a common quantum action yielding gravity and matter. The new direction instead asks whether quantum evolution itself fails through a field transition, while macroscopic geometry remains close to GR. That is a meaningful change of program. Candidate 0 may serve as a field laboratory, but quantizing it in the ordinary way does not automatically implement this alternative program. CEV calculations remain separate until an explicit bridge exists.
