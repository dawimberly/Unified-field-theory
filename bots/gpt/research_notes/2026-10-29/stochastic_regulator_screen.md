# Physical-regulator branch: stochastic composite gravity

Research checkpoint, October 29, 2026. Literature screening, not an adopted replacement for the current quantum model.

## Correction to the research map

The statement that a physical regulator is still missing from the older CEV calculations should not be read as saying no candidate has been proposed. Joshua Erlich's stochastic-composite-gravity program explicitly proposes one. Its existence changes the work queue: inspect the construction before inventing another regulator.

The 2022 author manuscript, A First Analysis of Stochastic Composite Gravity (arXiv:2208.10268), describes discrete stochastic fluctuations and numerical relaxation toward a flat composite-metric equilibrium. A later paper is listed as The Minkowski vacuum of stochastic composite gravity, Modern Physics Letters A 39 (2024), 2450179, DOI 10.1142/S0217732324501797. The publisher page was inaccessible; the author ORCID/Crossref metadata and publication record were checked. The inspected 2022 manuscript is not asserted to be identical to the final journal text.

The 2025 author essay, Predictions of Stochastic Composite Gravity (arXiv:2504.03084), specifies Poisson-distributed events, Gaussian field fluctuations, drift functions, and an equilibrium connection to quantum wavefunctionals. This is a substantive microscopic proposal, not merely a different numerical integration rule. The essay is inspected as an author manuscript; journal metadata alone is not a verification of every claim in the final published version.

## Three discriminating checks from existing results

### 1. Lorentz-invariant discreteness: retain, with qualifications

Bombelli, Henson and Sorkin, Discreteness without symmetry breaking: a theorem, Modern Physics Letters A 24 (2009), 2579-2587, DOI 10.1142/S0217732309031958, supplies an established result about Poisson sprinklings in Minkowski space. Under the theorem's measurable/equivariant construction assumptions, a sprinkling cannot intrinsically select a spacetime direction. The paper also excludes an associated Lorentz-invariant finite-valency graph. Thus a fixed nearest-neighbor lattice is not an interchangeable implementation of the proposed stochastic events. The theorem addresses kinematics, not gravitational dynamics or unitarity.

https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0605006

### 2. Deriving quantum mechanics: do not assume local stochastic equations suffice

Wallstrom, Inequivalence between the Schrodinger equation and the Madelung hydrodynamic equations, Physical Review A 49 (1994), 1613, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.49.1613, identifies a missing quantization condition in reconstructing quantum mechanics from the hydrodynamic equations. The publisher abstract was inspected; its full proof was not retrieved. This is a required test for a claimed stochastic derivation, not a demonstrated refutation of Erlich's specific model.

https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.49.1613

Our applicability inference: constructing a stochastic drift from an already admissible quantum wavefunctional is different from deriving the global quantum state space. The latter must explain phase/circulation quantization and behavior around nodes. The 2025 essay explicitly writes its drift in terms of a wavefunctional's phase and amplitude; that reverse construction does not by itself settle the forward derivation. A text search for Wallstrom returned no match in the two inspected manuscripts. This absence does not prove that the issue is unaddressed elsewhere.

### 3. Flat equilibrium: do not promote it into a full spectrum result

The displayed equilibrium evidence in the early stochastic work is not a calculation of the complete gauge-invariant spin-0 and spin-2 spectrum of the regulated model. To connect to our previous scalar-pole concern, the actual regulated, source-matched response must be derived. Substituting a cutoff scale into the old dimensionally continued kernel is not such a derivation. Nor does an equilibrium metric expectation prove that all perturbations have positive norm and stable time evolution.

## Next bounded tasks, without a foundational choice from the user

1. Treat stochastic composite gravity as a comparison branch. Preserve the ordinary constrained-QFT branch; do not silently replace its axioms.
2. Identify which stochastic equations are postulated and which are derived, and whether the permitted phase sectors are specified independently of a supplied quantum wavefunctional.
3. Identify the continuum/equilibrium limit in which the already published graviton calculation is claimed to apply. Mark any interchange of stochastic averaging, coarse graining and large N.
4. Seek an explicit regulated two-point/four-point response with physical source insertions. If absent, record an actual missing calculation rather than assert that equilibrium cures the pole.
5. Only if adopting this stochastic microscopic theory becomes necessary for the main proposal is a foundational human modeling choice required. Investigating its published properties does not yet require that choice.
